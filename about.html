Arjun Mehta began photographing wildlife at nineteen, after a monsoon season spent tracking leopards in the Western Ghats with his grandfather's battered Nikon F3. That single encounter — a black leopard crossing a flooded ridge at dawn — set the trajectory of a career that has since carried him from the tundra of Svalbard to the coral reefs of the Maldives, from the salt flats of the Atacama to the mangroves of the Sundarbans.

His work is defined by patience and restraint. Mehta rarely uses bait, drones, or playback; instead he builds trust through long, silent observation, often waiting days for a single frame. The result is a body of work that reads less like a portfolio and more like a field journal — intimate, unposed, and quietly urgent.

He has served as a visual consultant for WWF-India, the Royal Society for the Protection of Birds, and the IUCN Red List Programme, and his images have been used in conservation campaigns that directly funded the protection of over 40,000 hectares of habitat.
